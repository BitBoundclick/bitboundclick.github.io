Yes — Elden Ring is still worth playing in 2026, and it is the best entry point into FromSoftware's games for a new player, precisely because it is the most forgiving one they have made. ## What Elden Ring actually is
Elden Ring (FromSoftware, 2022) is an open-world action RPG set in the Lands Between, a broken fantasy world you explore largely on foot and on horseback as a Tarnished seeking the Elden Ring. The story, co-created with George R. R. Martin, is told mostly through item descriptions, NPC dialogue and the world itself — not cutscenes. You will finish the game with theories, not a tidy plot summary, and that is part of the appeal.

Combat is deliberate: stamina management, dodge timing, blocking, parrying, magic, bows, and dozens of weapon types. There is no difficulty slider. The difficulty *system* is the open world itself.

## Gameplay: hard, but fair — and flexible
This is the key point beginners miss. Elden Ring is not a corridor where Boss #3 must be beaten before you can progress. If a boss is too hard:

1. Ride somewhere else. The map is huge and most of it is open from the start.
2. Level up, upgrade your weapon, find better armor and talismans.
3. Use Spirit Ashes (AI summons) and co-op summons — the game gives you these tools on purpose.
4. Come back 10 levels later and win comfortably.

That loop — explore, get stronger, return — is the whole game, and it is genuinely addictive. A "build" matters more than reflexes: a mage, a bleed katana build, a heavy strength build, and a faith build all play like different games.

The one real frustration is the lack of a quest log. NPCs give vague directions and then move. If you play in short bursts with long gaps, you will forget who wanted what. Keep a small note, or accept that missing a side quest on a first run is normal.

## Graphics and performance in 2026
On PS5, Xbox Series X|S and a decent PC, Elden Ring looks stunning in its art direction — vast plains, burning battlefields, underground cities — even if its textures are not cutting-edge. Performance is far better than at launch: the notorious PC stuttering is largely fixed, and 60fps is the norm on current hardware. On PS4/Xbox One and low-end PCs, expect longer loads and lower frame rates, but the game remains fully playable.

## Story: brilliant if you like piecing things together
If you need a game to explain its plot clearly, Elden Ring will annoy you. If you enjoy lore videos, item text and environmental storytelling, it is a goldmine. The world feels like it existed before you arrived and will keep decaying after you leave. That atmosphere — lonely, beautiful, hostile — is unmatched.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

**Buy it if you:** like exploration, like learning boss patterns, enjoy RPG builds, or want a 60-100 hour game that respects your intelligence.

**Skip it (for now) if you:** want a guided story, hate dying and retrying, or only have 20 minutes at a time and want constant progress markers. Try a shorter Soulslike or a story-driven RPG first.

**Best value tip:** Wait for a sale — Elden Ring is frequently discounted, and the Shadow of the Erdtree expansion is worth adding only after you have finished, or nearly finished, the base game.

## Beginner tips that actually help
- **Vigor first.** Put early levels into Vigor (health) to 30-40 before chasing damage stats. Most new-player deaths are one-shot deaths.
- **Upgrade one weapon.** Do not spread smithing stones across five weapons. Pick one you enjoy and upgrade it.
- **Use the horse.** Torrent is not just transport — mounted combat lets you hit-and-run many open-world enemies safely.
- **Do not fight the Tree Sentinel first.** That golden knight at the start is a lesson, not a first boss: walk past him. Come back later.
- **Summon.** Spirit Ashes and co-op are part of the game, not cheating. Use them.

## FAQ

**Is Elden Ring too hard for beginners?**
It is challenging, but it is FromSoftware's most beginner-friendly game because the open world lets you leave a hard fight, level up, and return. The difficulty is in learning, not in reflexes alone.

**How long is Elden Ring?**
A first playthrough is typically 60-100 hours for the main path plus major side content. Completionists can pass 150 hours.

**Do I need to play Dark Souls first?**
No. Elden Ring is standalone. Dark Souls experience helps with combat habits, but it is not required.

**Is Elden Ring worth it without the DLC?**
Yes. The base game alone is a complete, enormous experience. Buy the DLC later if you want more of the same world at a higher difficulty.
